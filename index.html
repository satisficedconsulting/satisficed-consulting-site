The name

sat·is·ficed

verb — decision theory

To choose the option that's good enough to meet the need — deliberately, with judgment — rather than exhausting time and resources chasing a theoretical perfect one.

Coined by Nobel laureate Herbert Simon, blending "satisfy" and "suffice." It's not settling. It's the discipline of knowing exactly where more analysis stops paying for itself — and shipping the decision that works, on time.

What I do

Three moves. No detours.

Every engagement follows the same sequence — because skipping a step is how transformations stall, and lingering on one is how they miss the window.

01 — Assess

Find the real constraint

Map the operating model as it actually runs. Identify the one or two bottlenecks that are actually costing time and money — and ignore the rest for now.

02 — Automate

Build what removes the drag

Design and ship the workflow, integration, or tool that eliminates the manual step — sized to the problem, not to what looks impressive in a deck.

03 — Deliver

Run it through to outcome

Programme-manage the rollout end to end so the improvement survives contact with the org, not just the pilot.
